## 1. In short

## 2. People who take part in studies

Coders and participants don't need an account and don't accept these
terms. They take part on the basis of what the researcher told them. What
the service stores about them is in the
[privacy statement](privacy-statement.md).

- You log in through `[LOGIN PROVIDER]`. Keep your login safe and use a

- Give colleagues their own role in a study rather than sharing a login.

## 4. What you may use the service for

Research, teaching, and trying out the software. You may not:

- break the law, or use the service to harm, harass or deceive people
  beyond what an ethics committee approved for your study;
- collect data from people without telling them what they take part in
  and who is responsible, in the way your institution and the law
  require;
- upload material you have no right to use, or material that is illegal
  to hold or show;
- use it to distribute content unrelated to a study (file hosting, spam,
  phishing pages, advertising);
- try to get around limits, access other people's studies, test the
  service's security without our written permission, or overload it on
  purpose;
- resell the service or present it as your own.

## 5. Your responsibilities as a researcher

You, or your institution, are the *controller* of the personal data in
your studies; we are your *processor* (privacy statement §3). So it is
for you to:

1. **Have a lawful basis and inform participants.** Tell them who you
   are, what you collect (including response times, which are always
   recorded), why, for how long, and how to withdraw. The help topic
   "Privacy and where data lives" in the app lists what the service
   stores.
2. **Follow your institution's rules**: ethics review where it is
   required, your data management plan, and any registration of the
   study.
3. **Decide whether this server fits your data.** Check who runs it and
   where it is hosted (the server's information page). For sensitive
   data, such as health, political opinions, or data on children or
   vulnerable people, `[CHOOSE: "do not use this server; use one run by
   your own institution" / "contact us first"]`.
4. **Handle requests from participants**, including requests to delete
   their answers, which the service shows you. Do so within a reasonable
   time.
5. **Mind what you embed.** Images, audio and video loaded from other
   websites expose participants' IP addresses to those sites.
6. **Keep your own copy.** Download results and export studies you want
   to keep (§8).
7. **Pay and treat participants fairly**, where that applies. Agreements
   with panels, crowd platforms and coders are yours.

If your institution needs a signed agreement, contact us.

## 6. Your content

You keep all rights to your studies, units, designs and results. You
allow us to store, copy and show them as far as needed to run the
service for you (including backups). We don't look into the content of
studies, except when you ask for help, to investigate a suspected breach
of these terms or a security problem, or when the law requires it.
In the app, admins of the server can see accounts and how much studies
hold, not their content, unless they are given a role in the study. The
people who maintain the server itself can technically reach the database;
they are named in the privacy statement or the data processing terms.

## 7. Limits

Accounts have limits on the number of studies, units, coders and answers
(the app shows your use). Uploads, single units and single answers are
limited in size. We can change limits,
and raise them on request where capacity allows.

- Maintenance happens, with short interruptions, where possible
  announced `[WHERE]` and at quiet hours.
- **Plan for interruptions.** Don't schedule data collection so that a
  few hours of downtime would ruin it, and keep your own copies of

- We may change or stop the service. If we stop it, we tell account
  holders by email at least `[PERIOD, e.g. three months]` ahead, so you

  study to another AnnoTinder server.

## Notes for the reviewer (not part of the terms)

1. **Who is "we"?** See [`README.md`](README.md), decision 1. The
   liability and law clauses depend on it.
2. **Liability (§10)** is the clause that most needs a lawyer. Consumer
   law is unlikely to apply (researchers act professionally), but check.
   A university may have standard wording for services it offers to
   outsiders.
3. **How researchers accept the terms.** Today the app doesn't ask, and
   it has no place to show them: the server's information page that §5
   refers to is planned, not built (release plan, P-A3). Until then the
   terms are sent to each researcher who is let in. A recorded acceptance
   at the first login as a researcher, and again when the terms change,
   is better, and needed if the data processing terms are to be agreed
   this way (`docs/plans/go-live.md`, P-12).
4. **Sensitive data (§5.3)** is a real choice. Saying "not on the public
   server" keeps the risk and the paperwork small, and gives the
   self-hosted option a clear purpose.
5. **What the app already does** to back these terms (checked in the
   code, November 2026): admins can block a user and suspend a study (a
   suspended study stays readable and downloadable, and can be deleted);
   plans and limits per account; deleting a study or coders' data,
   resetting answers and exporting a study need a recent login with a
   second factor; study export for moving to another server; a user can
   delete their own account, and an admin a user's, with the studies only
   that user has a role in (§9; `docs/accounts.md` §5). **Not yet:** the
   app sends no email, so every "we tell you by email" in these terms is
   done by hand.
6. **The lab's server** can do with a short "house rules" version: §4,
   §5 and §8.
